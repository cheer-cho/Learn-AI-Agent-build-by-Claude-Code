# Dress Code Policy

## Purpose

TechCorp's dress code is designed to be simple, inclusive, and practical. We hire adults, and we trust employees to dress in a way that reflects well on themselves and the company. This policy sets expectations while leaving room for personal expression, cultural and religious dress, and role-specific requirements.

## Default Standard: Business Casual

The company-wide default is **business casual**. In practice this means clean, well-maintained clothing that would be appropriate if a customer or business partner unexpectedly walked through the office. Examples of appropriate attire include collared shirts, blouses, sweaters, chinos, skirts, dresses, and closed or smart casual footwear.

The following are not appropriate in any TechCorp workplace: clothing with offensive slogans or imagery, beachwear, athletic wear worn as a full outfit (gym shorts, tank tops), and visibly damaged or unclean clothing. Safety-sensitive areas such as labs and warehouse floors have additional requirements posted on site, which take precedence over this policy.

## Jeans at Headquarters

**Jeans (denim) are allowed at TechCorp headquarters**, provided they are clean and free of rips or heavy distressing. The one exception: **jeans are not permitted during client meetings**, whether the meeting takes place at headquarters or at a client site. If your calendar includes a client-facing meeting, plan your outfit accordingly or keep a change of clothes at your desk.

Regional offices may adopt stricter local standards where market norms require it; check with your site lead if you are visiting another office for client work.

## Client-Facing Roles and Events

Employees in sales, consulting, and executive-facing roles should default to the more formal end of business casual on days with external meetings. For trade shows, conferences, and formal company events, the event organizer will communicate a specific dress standard in advance — typically business formal or branded company attire.

## Remote Work and Video Calls

TechCorp does **not maintain a dress code for remote work**, with one expectation: employees appearing on video calls — internal or external — should present themselves professionally on camera. What you wear off camera at home is your business. For external video calls with clients or partners, treat the call as you would an in-person client meeting.

## Religious and Medical Accommodations

Nothing in this policy overrides dress associated with religious observance, cultural identity, or medical needs. Employees who need an accommodation or believe the policy conflicts with a protected practice should contact HR; accommodation requests are handled confidentially.

## Enforcement

Managers should address dress-code concerns privately and respectfully. Repeated, deliberate disregard of this policy is handled through the standard performance process. Questions about edge cases can be sent to your HR Business Partner — when in doubt, dress one notch more formal than you think you need.
